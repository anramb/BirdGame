Sound ID is a bird sound identification tool that uses acoustic analysis together with geographic information to help identify birds from their calls and songs.

BirdNET provides the acoustic identification. Sound ID combines the acoustic prediction with geographic information to help determine which results are relevant to the selected recording location.

Geographic information provides additional context; it does not replace the acoustic identification.

BirdNET analyses the recorded sound and produces an acoustic score for possible bird species.

The percentage shown is an acoustic model score. A 50% score does not mean there is a 50% certainty that the bird is present.

Acoustic results can be affected by:

  • recording quality
  • background noise
  • distance from the bird
  • overlapping bird calls
  • other environmental sounds

BirdNET predictions are identification assistance and should not automatically be treated as confirmed observations.

BirdNET acknowledgement

Sound ID uses BirdNET technology; it is not an official BirdNET application and is not endorsed by BirdNET, Cornell Lab, or Chemnitz University of Technology.

Recommended citation: Kahl, S., Wood, C. M., Eibl, M., & Klinck, H. (2021). BirdNET: A deep learning solution for avian diversity monitoring. Ecological Informatics, 61, 101236.

Light: Shows more possibilities. Lower acoustic scores may still be shown when there is meaningful geographic evidence supporting the species.

Normal: Provides a balance between acoustic results and geographic relevance.

Strict: Shows only strong acoustic matches of 50% or higher that also have supporting current local evidence for the selected location.

Example: If BirdNET gives a species an acoustic score of 50%, Strict mode can show it only when the available geographic evidence also supports the species locally.

Higher acoustic scores represent stronger acoustic matches, but they are not direct percentages of certainty.

Sound ID can use bird records from the South African Bird Atlas Project (SABAP2) to provide geographic context for BirdNET predictions.

SABAP2 contains bird observations collected through bird atlas surveys. The region is divided into geographic areas called pentads.

A SABAP2 pentad is a 5 × 5 minute geographic grid cell. When a recording location or SABAP2 pentad is available, Sound ID can compare a BirdNET prediction with available geographic evidence.

SABAP2 does not change the BirdNET acoustic score. Geographic evidence provides additional context that helps determine whether an acoustic prediction is geographically relevant.

Official SABAP2 project

The same bird sound can have different geographic significance depending on where and when it was recorded.

  • Automatic / GPS
  • Country
  • Province
  • Southern Africa
  • No location filtering
  • Recording latitude/longitude
  • SABAP2 Pentad

For an older recording, provide the location where the recording was actually made. Use that recording location rather than your current phone location.

  1. Record or upload a sound. Record a bird call/song or upload an existing recording.
  2. Choose the recording location. Use GPS, select a geographic context, or provide a SABAP2 Pentad where appropriate.
  3. Choose a result mode. Light, Normal or Strict.
  4. Read the acoustic score. The percentage is BirdNET’s acoustic model score.
  5. Check the geographic status. The message below the result describes the available geographic evidence.
  6. Listen and compare. Use the available playback/reference options to compare the sound.
  7. Verify unusual results. Listen again and, where possible, confirm the bird visually or using another reliable source.

Sound ID cannot guarantee an identification.

  • BirdNET acoustic identification technology.
  • SABAP2 geographic occurrence context.
  • Xeno-canto reference audio where applicable.
  • Project canonical taxonomy, reference images, audio and associated per-record credits included in the application data.
