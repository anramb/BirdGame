About Chirp Coach

Chirp Coach is a tool to help you learn and identify southern African bird calls. It includes several modes, each designed for a different stage of the learning process.

Modes

Single Bird Select

Browse and listen to individual birds one at a time. Use this mode to familiarise yourself with a specific bird's call. You can filter by species group (e.g. Cisticola, Kingfisher, Lark) to quickly find related birds, scroll through the full list or type in the bird name. When typing the bird name, you can type any part of an English or Afrikaans bird name without any spaces to quickly find the bird you are looking for. For example, type tba to find "Chinspot Batis" or lkl to find "Bandkeelkleinjantjie". Each bird shows a dynamic spectrogram, audio player, and a photo (if available).

Learning Mode

Play through a filtered playlist of birds. You can filter by bird (see a dropdown list of birds available), bird group (e.g. Cisticolas, Raptors, Seedeaters), hotspot (birding location), habitat, and difficulty level (Beginner, Intermediate, Advanced). The playlist plays each bird in sequence, showing the name, spectrogram, and audio. Great for systematic study before a birding trip.If the spectrogram fails to load, click on the Retry button.

🎙️ Bird Recording Studio

Record high-quality field recordings of birds directly from your phone. The studio is designed for wildlife recording rather than voice calls, and requests that the microphone's automatic processing (echo cancellation, noise suppression, automatic gain control) be switched off so that the natural bird sound is preserved as accurately as possible. All recordings are saved as uncompressed WAV files at 48 kHz, 24-bit PCM – the format recommended by the Macaulay Library and eBird for archival submissions.

While recording, a large live spectrogram is displayed with a frequency scale in kHz. A signal quality indicator continuously estimates the signal-to-noise ratio and warns you if the bird signal is weak compared with background noise, or if clipping is detected. An elapsed-time indicator encourages recordings of the recommended 15–30 seconds.

After stopping, the complete waveform and a full spectrogram are displayed. You can:

  • Drag to select any region on the waveform or spectrogram.
  • ✂ Remove selection – cut out that section (e.g. handling noise) and keep the rest.
  • Keep selection only – discard everything outside the selection.
  • Make multiple successive edits before saving.
  • Reset to original at any time – the original recording is never modified.

A recording quality summary is shown after stopping, including duration, sample rate, bit depth, peak and average level, estimated SNR, and whether clipping was detected. You can add optional metadata (species name, location, date, time, notes) before saving. Files are named automatically with the date and time, e.g. LaughingDove_20260814_083215.wav and LaughingDove_20260814_083215_edit.wav for edited versions. If your browser supports the File System Access API, you can choose where the file is saved; otherwise it downloads automatically.

Tip: For the best field recording: record for 15–30 seconds, move as close to the bird as possible, point the microphone toward it, avoid wind, and keep the phone still. A WAV recording saved from this studio can be uploaded directly to an eBird checklist for archiving in the Macaulay Library.
Soundscape ID Training

Listen to real-life field recordings containing multiple bird species. Try to identify each bird you hear in the soundscape. This mode simulates the actual birding experience where many birds call simultaneously.

Game Mode

Test your knowledge. A bird call plays and you must choose the correct answer from four options (A, B, C, D). Answers are randomised, so you cannot simply memorise positions. You can filter by group, hotspot, or level. See if you can beat your own high score and how long your streak can be. At the end of a round, birds you answered incorrectly are listed so you can focus on them.

Multiplayer Quiz

Challenge your friends (or an AI opponent) to a bird identification showdown. Supports 1–6 players on the same device in pass-and-play style. Choose your hotspot, target score, and time limit. Each turn, a player picks a difficulty level (Easy, Medium, Hard, or Bonus). Question types include sound identification, photo identification, and spectrogram matching. Bonus rounds show only a spectrogram with no sound – identify the bird from the visual pattern alone! After answering, the correct bird's call and spectrogram are shown for learning. A built-in AI player can join if you want to practise solo.

Super Spectro Challenge

Put your spectrogram reading skills to the ultimate test! In this mode, you are shown only a spectrogram – no audio is played. Identify the bird purely from the visual pattern of the spectrogram. Filter by group, hotspot, or level. This is the hardest challenge and will sharpen your ability to recognise birds from their spectrograms alone.

📦 Offline Packs

Download bird sound packs for specific regions so you can use the app without an internet connection – perfect for the bush! The app shell (pages, bird data, metadata) is cached automatically. In the Offline Packs page you can browse available packs grouped by region (e.g. Kruger National Park, KZN, Western Cape). Each pack shows the number of species, audio files, and estimated download size. Once downloaded, you can use Learn, Game, and other modes offline with those birds. You can update or remove packs at any time. When you add new birds to a region in the future, simply re-download the pack to get the updated audio.

Filters Explained

Species (Single Bird Select)

The species filter groups birds by the first word of their English name (e.g. all Cisticola species, all Kingfisher species). This makes it easy to compare similar-sounding birds within a family.

Bird Group

Birds are organised into broad groups such as Cisticolas, Garden and Woodland, Groundbirds, LBJs (Little Brown Jobs), Raptors, Seedeaters, Waders, and more.

Hotspot

Filter by birding location to study the birds you are likely to encounter at a specific site, for example Kruger National Park, Isimangaliso, Drakensberg & Sani Pass, Caprivi/Chobe, etc. For specials, select the Hotspot with "Special". "Other" indicates more general birds you will encounter in a specific Hotspot.

Habitat

Filter by habitat type (Forest, Woodland/Savanna, Grassland, Wetland/Marsh, Fynbos, etc.) to focus on birds typical of a specific environment.

Level

  • 1 Beginner – Common, distinctive calls that are easy to recognise.
  • 2 Intermediate – Calls that require some experience to identify.
  • 3 Advanced – Subtle, uncommon, or easily confused calls for experienced birders.

Spectrograms

Each bird displays a scrollable spectrogram – a visual representation of the sound. Time runs along the horizontal axis and frequency (pitch) along the vertical axis. Darker colours indicate louder sounds. The red line tracks your playback position. You can click anywhere on the spectrogram to jump to that point in the recording. When stopping or pausing the call, you can scroll on the spectrogram to view the call.

Language

You can switch between English and Afrikaans bird names using the language selector on each page.

Tips for Learning Bird Calls

Tip: Start with Beginner level in Learning Mode or Game Mode. Select a Hotspot and Habitat you are familiar with, for example "Wider Gauteng 100km/Nylsvley Other" and "Garden". Once you are confident with those birds, move to Intermediate, then Advanced.
Tip: Use the species filter in Single Bird Select to compare similar species side by side (e.g. all Cisticolas, all Robin-chats).
Tip: Before a birding trip, filter by the hotspot you plan to visit so you know which birds to expect. Play these birds in the background while you are working or relaxing.
Tip: Pay attention to the spectrogram shape – over time you will learn to recognise certain patterns even before you identify the sound.
